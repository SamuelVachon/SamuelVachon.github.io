# Introduction

The idea of myHealth is incepted to help people with chronic illnesses manage the challenges that comes with it. The challenges can range from medications to doctor appointments and tracking their medical data. Forgetting the medication can lead to serious health issues. Long threads of emails with the doctor's office can be a headache to navigate trough. Having to track their data in all sorts of different apps can be confusing at times. myHealth stands as a centralized tool for all of these activities. In a context where more people are being prescribed adhd medications[^1] or with diabetes[^2], helping those people overcome their conditions is that more important.

## Analysis

In the light of those interviews, the needs that seems to affect the most people are the four following. First one is a need for reminders, second is a need for easier appointment scheduling, third is a need for easier contact methods with health professionals and fourth is a need for information.

First, most people interviewed reported that they don't often forget to take their medication. Two stated that they rarely ever forget and 2 stated they forget about once a week. The first thought would be to discard this idea. However, when they do forget about it, some are highly affected by it. In average, they rated the impact it has on their day at a 6.25 on a scale of one to ten. Two of them even stating it affects them at an 8 level. So, even though they don't forget often, when they do forget, it affects them highly, creating a need for efficient tracking and reminders to take their medications.

Second, as we can see in their answers, the way to contact their doctors can vary from people to people. One contact through email, one doesn't have a doctor and two must make a phone call and potentially wait on the line. In average, they rate at a level of 5/10 the easiness to contact their healthcare professionals. Thus, by having a text message option, the access to doctors would be greatly eased for them, and it would be waiting time on the phone or long email threads. Also, for someone that doesn't have a doctor, an option could be added to chat with myHealth's own professionals.

Third, the reason all of them contact professionals is either to take an appointment or request information's. One quoted "Online appointment booking" has a must-have tool for an application whose target it is to help people with chronic health conditions. Therefore, the options to book appointments are very important. Also, a lot of the information about medications can be answer through pills information documents, that could be made available through the app.

# Usability Testing

By looking at the user flow chart, 6 sub-flows can be identified. For the purpose of this testing plan, they will be names Log In and Registration, Add a Reminder, Confirm or Forget the Pill, Request an appointment, Add a Contact to message and Message an existing contact. For consistency, the user should start the tests at the home page, expect when the context limits it. The goal is to see if they divert from the expected or shortest paths and behaviors.

## Log In and Registration

Task 1: Registration

Context: First time user, enter the app on the login page.

Expected behavior:

i.  Recognized they don't have an account

ii. Click the "Register" Button

iii. Enter their information

iv. Click "Register"

Task 2: Log In

Context: Enter the app at the login page.

Expected behavior:

i.  Enter the Log In information previously registered

ii. Click "Log In"

Task 3: Log Out

Expected behavior:

i.  Click the profile page in the bottom menu

ii. Click Log Out

## Add a Reminder

Task 4: Add a Reminder once a day

Expected behavior:

i.  Recognized the "plus" button as the way to add a reminder and click it.

ii. Enter the name of the pill or the name of the condition

iii. Select the appropriate pill

iv. Enter the time of the reminder

v.  Click "Done"

Task 4: Add a Reminder twice a day

Expected behavior:

i.  Recognized the "plus" button as the way to add a reminder and click it.

ii. Enter the name of the pill or the name of the condition

iii. Select the appropriate pill

iv. Enter the time of the reminder

v.  Recognized the "plus" button as the way to add another timeslot and click it.

vi. Enter the second time

vii. Click "Done"

## Confirm or Forgot the pill

Task 5: You took your pill

Expected behavior:

i.  Click "Taken"

Task 6: You didn't take your pill

Expected behavior:

i.  Click "Forgot"

## Request an appointment  {#request-an-appointment}

Task 7: Request first appointment

Expected behavior:

i.  Click the calendar button on the bottom menu

ii. Click the "Plus" Button

iii. Enter the reason of the appointment

iv. Click "Done"

Task 8: Request an appointment when others are already there

Expected behavior:

i.  Click the calendar button on the bottom menu

ii. Click the "Plus" Button

iii. Enter the reason of the appointment

iv. Click "Done"

## Add a Contact to message

Task 9: Add a doctor to the contact list

Expected Behavior:

i.  Click the messaging button on the bottom menu

ii. Click the "new message" icon on the upper right

iii. Enter the name of the doctor

iv. Click on the name of the doctor

## Message an existing contact

Task 10: Send a message to an existing contact

Expected Behavior:

i.  Click the messaging button on the bottom menu

ii. Click the contact

iii. Write and send a message

Task 11: Use our quick chat

Expected Behavior:

i.  Click the messaging button on the bottom menu

ii. Click the "myHealth QuickChat" contact

iii. Write and send a message

# Reflection

The design process was incredibly useful at detecting what the user needs in the application. Instead of using my own opinion, I got to know from the people who used the app the feature that should be included. Also, iterating through sketches, wireframes, storyboard and finally prototype, really help correcting mistakes I would usually in term of usability. At multiple points, I encountered an option that was not easy to understand why it is there. With those, I have instant feedback on what could be changed without having spent too much time on it yet like it would've been in the implementation phase.

The most challenging thing about this project was my poor knowledge of the tools used in UX design. I've never used any before. Luckly, Figma offers free premium accounts to undergraduate students and a lot of beginner friendly tools and asset library so that my vision could be made reality.
